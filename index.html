SITUATION

Clinical trials relied on paper-based patient assessments — slow, error-prone data collection, 8–12 week site activation, and inconsistent patient compliance with no structured way to understand why patients disengaged.

TASK

Design and deliver a digital, patient-centric assessment platform: replace paper with a compliant real-time system, ground design in direct patient/caregiver feedback, and cut setup timelines without compromising regulatory validation.

ACTION
  • Research loop: Embedded In-Trial Interviews into product discovery, translating friction points into workflow changes.
  • Library-based build: Leveraged 600+ pre-approved assessments and 2,500+ pre-built eCOA forms instead of building from scratch.
  • Unified testing: Consolidated separate eCOA and IRT UAT cycles into one process.
  • Cross-functional alignment: Partnered with clinical science, regulatory, and site ops to meet FDA patient-experience data expectations.
BOTTOM LINE

Grounding decisions in direct patient feedback and re-architecting setup around a pre-validated library model delivered measurable gains in both patient experience and operations — without compromising regulatory rigor.

Key challenges & trade-offs (9)
Speed vs. regulatory rigor
Risk-tiered acceleration — library instruments move fast, custom elements get full validation; regulatory involved early as a design partner.
Turning interviews into requirements
Only acted on friction points recurring across multiple interviews/sites, triangulated with quantitative signals.
Conflicting stakeholders
Made trade-offs explicit and win-condition-based; defined success criteria per stakeholder upfront.
Standardization vs. custom needs
Defaulted to the library; required specific clinical justification to deviate (e.g. Sickle Cell Disease population needed a custom instrument).
16-country, multi-language complexity
Designed for the hardest case — lowest literacy, most constrained user — first; centralized linguistic validation.
BYOD vs. provisioned devices
Segmented by population: BYOD for tech-comfortable patients, provisioned devices for elderly/low-connectivity groups.
Proving attribution
Used phased rollout by site/cohort where possible; stayed explicit about which levers most plausibly contributed.
Change management at sites
Involved site coordinators early in design and pilot, not just training, with a real feedback channel during rollout.
Timeline pressure compounding risk
Front-loaded the riskiest unknowns so problems surfaced early with time to fix.
This project taught me that in regulated environments, patient-centered design and compliance aren't in tension — the fastest path through validation is often the one built on real patient evidence, not around it.

Modernizing the premium grocery supply chain with AI

Self-directed exercise

AI Demand Forecasting — Good Food Holdings

Role: Principal Product Manager (exercise) — Internal Enterprise & Retail Tech
This case study uses a real company's public structure (five independently run grocery banners) as the setting for a self-directed strategic exercise demonstrating product judgment. SITUATION

Five independently branded premium grocery banners, each with distinct culture and go-to-market approach, ran on disconnected legacy inventory systems. Store teams manually guessed daily order quantities, causing waste and stockouts.

TASK

Reduce food waste and stockouts using AI-driven demand forecasting — without imposing a one-size-fits-all system that erodes each banner's local identity or frontline manager trust.

ACTION
  • Reframed the problem: centralize the data plumbing without centralizing the decisions that give each banner its identity.
  • Build vs. buy: bought the forecasting engine, kept feature engineering and data ownership in-house.
  • Designed for trust: explainable-AI interface, bounded override threshold (±20%), non-punitive accuracy view after a real trust-breaking incident in pilot.
  • Phased rollout: Alpha (1 store) → Beta (2 contrasting banners) → GA — each phase framed as a hypothesis to test.
The moment trust broke — and the fix
A store manager overrode the AI's recommendation based on 15 years of experience — and was wrong, publicly losing ~$600 in sales. The fix wasn't better AI accuracy. It was replacing visible override "scorecards" with a non-judgmental, aggregate accuracy view, so managers could trust the tool's track record without feeling surveilled on every decision.
RESULT

A staged rollout that protects each banner's identity while creating a shared forecasting layer — doubling as the GTM/launch strategy, since each phase gates go/no-go decisions on a hypothesis and expands cross-functional readiness as scope grows.

Presenting projected benchmarks as hypotheses to validate — rather than confirmed results — is itself part of the product judgment this exercise demonstrates.
// CASE STUDY 03 — HACKATHON SUBMISSION

Hike Relaunch: the complete PM journey

From Play Store friction to a feature WhatsApp is structurally unwilling to build

This was built solo for a product hackathon centered on relaunching Hike (the Indian messaging app that wound down in September 2025). RESEARCH

Rather than starting from a feature idea, the process started with 58 live Google Play reviews of WhatsApp, clustered into nine recurring friction themes and scored on severity and whether WhatsApp could structurally copy a fix. Secondary research — verifying WhatsApp's 2026 username rollout and Communities privacy beta — disqualified two promising themes as non-defensible, leaving one theme with no live disqualification: the gap between speaking a language confidently and writing formal English.

SELECTED SOLUTION

Voice-to-Formal-English: speak naturally in any Indian language, review an AI-formalized English draft, then send — built for institutional moments (housing societies, schools, gig-platform disputes, government forms) where a user's English-writing confidence, not their English-speaking ability, decides whether they're taken seriously.

PRIORITIZATION & SCOPE
  • RICE-scored against alternatives: the winning feature scored 140 vs. 75 and 15 for two other candidates — winning on impact and confidence, not raw reach.
  • Vertical sequencing, also RICE-scored: Resident Welfare Associations (housing societies) selected to launch first over schools, gig platforms, and government portals — on WhatsApp-group density and a single well-scoped integration surface.
  • Full PRD: functional and non-functional requirements, explicit non-goals (no auto-send, ever), and a launch-readiness bar of 85%+ drafts sent with no or minor edits.
Roadmap, growth model & monetization
Phase 1 (Weeks 0–2)
Prove it in one vertical, one city — 3–5 friendly societies, manual coordination. KPI: 50+ formalized messages sent, 70%+ sent without edit.
Phase 2 (Month 1–3)
Expand template library and city count, first institutional-platform integration conversation, paid Individual-Certified tier goes live.
Phase 3 (Month 4–9)
Named institutional integrations live, correction dataset compounding per dialect, all three pricing layers active.
Monetization decided upfront
Hike previously raised $261M without deciding who pays. This plan fixes that: a permanently free individual tier as the acquisition funnel, a pay-per-use certified tier, and recurring institutional SaaS pricing for RWAs and schools — decided before Phase 1, not after.
Growth loop
Acquisition is structurally visible, not paid: a formalized message lands inside a real WhatsApp group, and "how did you write that so well?" becomes the referral loop by design.
The hardest part of this exercise wasn't picking a feature — it was ruling out ideas that felt strong on first read (a username system, hiding phone numbers) once secondary research showed WhatsApp was already shipping them. Real product judgment showed up more in what got cut than in what got built.
