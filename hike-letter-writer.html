Why It Failed

Six findings. None of them
read "bad product."

EXHIBIT A
The moat is the people, not the app
Messaging is winner-takes-most. Leaving WhatsApp doesn't mean leaving an app — it means leaving your mother, your landlord, your building group. Hike could win on features and still lose.
→ A better product does not beat a bigger contact list.
EXHIBIT B
Everything Hike built could be copied
Stickers, status, stories, payments, even an alias-based identity (Hike ID). Hike shipped several of these in India first. WhatsApp shipped its own version to 400 million Indians already inside it.
→ A feature is not a moat when your competitor has ten times your reach.
EXHIBIT C
Too many features, no clear identity
Messenger, stickers, news, wallet, payments, games, avatars, stories, mini-apps. Ask a user what Hike was and you'd get ten different answers.
→ A product that is ten things can be replaced one thing at a time.
EXHIBIT D
The problems Hike solved stopped existing
Free SMS, Hike Direct, data-light access — all built on Indian scarcity. Then Jio arrived, data became nearly free, and the scarcity disappeared.
→ The features weren't badly built. The problem simply vanished.
EXHIBIT E
No way to make money, decided too late
$261M raised, 100M users, no revenue plan. Investor money funded growth. Growth never funded the company. Revenue was always "for later."
→ Later never arrived. This brief decides pricing before Phase 1.
EXHIBIT F
Too many pivots, too fast
Super-app, then unbundling, Sticker Chat, HikeLand, Vibe, Rush. Each pivot made sense alone. Together they told everyone the company didn't know what it was.
→ No version of Hike lasted long enough for anyone to build a habit around it.
How WhatsApp Won

While Hike shipped ten things, WhatsApp shipped one: messages and photos, fast, on any network, with almost nothing else in the way. It never had to out-build Hike. It only had to already be where everyone's contacts were.

This brief's rule follows from that: don't out-feature WhatsApp. Find the fight it structurally will not enter.

The Opportunity Tree

Three friction clusters. One survived contact with reality.

Traced from live WhatsApp Play Store reviews, then pressure-tested against the doc's five real moats: switching cost, regulation, data network effect, supply side, or a user group the incumbent won't serve.

NORTH STAR — ship one feature WhatsApp cannot copy next quarter
Friction 01 — Identity & Privacy

Username instead of phone number

Real pain: a phone number is tied to your bank account and every stranger you message. Nine reviewers cited restriction/ban anxiety around it.

Ruled out
WhatsApp is already rolling out optional usernames through 2026, and already lets users hide their number inside Communities in beta. Hike tried this exact feature once before, as "Hike ID" — it didn't save the company then either.
Friction 02 — Feature Bloat

"Nothing added without permission"

Real pain: the un-removable Meta AI icon, a redesigned Updates tab, one-tap status replies sending by accident. Users call it "clogging the app."

Positioning, not a moat
Meta needs Channels, AI and engagement surfaces for its own roadmap — that's a real, structural weakness. But it's a brand stance to build the whole product around, not a single shippable feature. Carried forward as tone, not as the north-star build.
Friction 03 — Language & Formality

Voice, in your language → formal English

Real pain: confident speakers, unconfident writers, stuck communicating with schools, housing societies and offices who expect polished English.

Selected — build this
Passes on supply side (institutional integrations Meta won't chase) and on an underserved user group (older, non-metro, low-English-literacy) Meta's AI investment isn't built to prioritise.
Who This Is For

One job. Three doors it needs to open.

Same underlying need — sound confident and formal in a language you don't write easily — applied to whichever institution is on the other end of the message.

Resident, Housing Society

The Committee Letter

"I can say it perfectly. I just can't write it the way they'll take seriously."
Pain: A maintenance dispute or safety complaint needs a formal note to the RWA committee. Written in halting English, it gets ignored; spoken in Bengali, it never gets read at all.
Parent, School WhatsApp Group

The Reply To The Teacher

"I don't want to sound like I can't help my own child."
Pain: Fee reminders, permission slips and complaints arrive in formal English. Replying in kind, without embarrassment, is the actual barrier.
Gig Worker

The Dispute Message

"If I message the client wrong, I lose the booking, not just the argument."
Pain: A payment dispute or client complaint needs to sound professional fast, in the moment, not after ten minutes of typing and re-typing in a second language.
Elder, Family Group

The Government Form

"My daughter usually writes these for me."
Pain: Scheme applications and grievance filings demand written, formal English. Today that means depending on someone else being free and willing.
The Mechanism

From spoken Bengali to a letter the committee reads twice.

Nothing sends without the speaker seeing it first. That review step isn't friction — it's the entire reason this can be trusted with something as high-stakes as a formal complaint.

01 · SPEAK

Record in any language

User speaks naturally — dialect, code-mixing, informal phrasing, all fine. This is the one step that asks nothing extra of them.

02 · HEAR

Transcribe in-language

Speech-to-text renders what was actually said, in the original language, before any translation happens.

03 · REFRAME

Translate & formalise

Translated to English, then rewritten in the register the recipient expects — a complaint, a request, a notice.

04 · REVIEW

Shown back to the speaker

Editable draft, in full, before anything sends. Meaning-preservation is the user's call, not the model's.

05 · SEND

Delivered as a normal message

Reaches the RWA group, the teacher, the client — as a message from Hike, not a bot.

What was said

Why WhatsApp Won't Follow

Not "can't." Won't — and that's the more durable answer.

Meta could technically ship a translation feature next quarter. What it can't cheaply replicate is what makes this one actually useful.

Supply side

The institutions, not the AI

Getting a formal message actually acted on means integrating with RWA management tools, school admin systems, government grievance portals, gig-platform dispute flows — thousands of small, fragmented local bodies. Unglamorous, relationship-heavy work that moves zero needles on Meta's global metrics.

Underserved user

A segment Meta's AI isn't built for

Older, low-English-literacy, non-metro users producing formal correspondence is a tiny slice of engagement-per-user globally. Meta's AI investment is optimised for scale and general fluency, not for going deep on one narrow, unglamorous need.

Data, weaker but real

Every correction is a lesson Meta doesn't get

Each edit a user makes to the AI's formal draft teaches the real, dialect-specific way people phrase requests and complaints — a dataset general-purpose models don't have and have no reason to collect for a use case this narrow.

How It's Built

Nothing here needs inventing. It needs assembling well.

Every layer already exists as usable technology. The engineering work is the pipeline, the guardrails and the institutional integrations — not new AI research.

Speech-to-text
Whisper / Bhashini / AI4Bharat (IndicWhisper)
Bhashini is India's own national speech-translation mission — built for exactly this problem, and worth a direct integration conversation.
Translate + reframe
LLM API (GPT / Claude / Gemini / Llama)
Prompt-engineered for formal register, with instructions that preserve meaning over fluency — a hackathon-buildable step, not a research one.
Review & edit
Plain text editor, pre-send
The trust layer. Never optional, never skippable — this is what makes a wrong AI guess survivable.
Institutional link
RWA / school / gig-platform integration
Where the actual moat lives. Starts as one partnership per vertical, not a platform.
Known risks, not hidden

Dialect and code-mixed speech (Benglish, Hinglish) will have higher error rates than clean single-language input — exactly the users this is built for. Latency and per-message API cost need real budgeting. Voice data handling needs a privacy story that matches a product pitched on trust. None of these block a hackathon demo; all of them block a real launch if ignored.

How Hike Makes Money — Decided Now

Priced before Phase 1, not after $261M.

Layer 01

Freemium translation

Basic voice-to-formal-text is free — it's the acquisition hook, and it has to work for anyone. Certified formats (RTI applications, tenancy notices, school complaint templates) sit behind a small per-use fee.

Who pays: the individual, occasionally
Layer 02

Institutional integration fee

RWA management platforms, school administration systems and gig platforms pay to be the verified receiving channel for Hike-formalised messages — the same fee logic as any B2B2C integration.

Who pays: the institution, recurring
Layer 03

Public digitisation partnerships

Government grievance and scheme-application portals have their own digital-literacy mandates. Co-funded pilots turn a cost centre for them into a distribution channel for Hike.

Who pays: state/CSR budget lines, per pilot
Exhibit E, appliedHike raised $261M without ever answering who pays. This brief answers it on page one: individuals pay occasionally, institutions pay recurring, and the free tier stays free because it's the funnel, not the business.
Roadmap

Three phases, hackathon-honest.

Phase 1 · Demo-able in 48 hours

Speak → Formalise → Send, one vertical

Full voice-to-formal-English pipeline for a single use case — the housing-society complaint letter. Review-before-send live. This alone is the demo.

Phase 2 · Template & vertical expansion

School and gig-platform formats added

Certified templates library grows past RWA into school communication and gig disputes. First real institutional pilot conversation opened.

Phase 3 · The moat goes live

Institutional integrations, correction dataset, monetisation

Verified receiving channels live with named RWA platforms/schools, the correction dataset compounding per dialect, all three revenue layers active.
